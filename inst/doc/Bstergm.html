Bayesian separable temporal ERGMs for binary and signed networks

Bstergm fits Bayesian separable temporal ERGMs to sequences of binary networks, with bstergm(), and of signed networks, with bstergm_sign(). This vignette analyses the U.S. Senate data of Caimo and Gollini (2025): the signs with bstergm_sign() and the interactions, a sequence of binary networks, with bstergm().

The model

A signed network \(y_t\) on \(n\) nodes has ties \(y_{ij,t} \in \{-1, 0, +1\}\). It is written as an interaction layer \(x_t\) (\(x_{ij,t} = |y_{ij,t}|\)) and a sign layer \(z_t\) (\(z_{ij,t} = 1\) if \(y_{ij,t} = +1\)), which is only defined for interacting pairs.

As in the separable temporal ERGM of Krivitsky and Handcock (2014), each transition \(y_{t-1} \to y_t\) is split into a formation network \(y^F_t = y_{t-1} \cup y_t\) and a persistence network \(y^P_t = y_{t-1} \cap y_t\), for both layers. The model is \[ p(y_t \mid y_{t-1}) = \prod_{U \in \{F, P\}} p(x^U_t \mid y_{t-1}; \xi^U)\; p(z^U_t \mid x^U_t, y_{t-1}; \zeta^U), \] where every factor is an ERGM. The sign model \(p(z^U_t \mid x^U_t, \cdot\,; \zeta^U)\) lets the sign of every interacting dyad of \(x^U_t\) vary while the interactions stay fixed. Its log-odds of a positive sign are \(\zeta^{U\top} \Delta_{ij} s(z^U_t; x^U_t, y_{t-1})\). Under independent priors the posterior factorises into four independent parts: \(\zeta^F\), \(\zeta^P\), \(\xi^F\) and \(\xi^P\).

Signed-network statistics

The sign process is modelled on the positive ties of each formation or persistence network. The interacting pairs are fixed and stored with the network, so negative ties are the interacting pairs that are not positive. as_signed_network() builds this representation for a single network. Any ergm term then describes positive ties, and the signed terms of the package also use the negative ties:

Posterior sampling

bstergm_sign() samples the posterior of the formation and persistence parameters of the sign process with the approximate exchange algorithm. By default it uses adaptive Metropolis-Hastings. The two components are independent and run in parallel with cores, as do several chains. A short run looks like this:

f <- ~ edges + nodematch("Party", diff = TRUE, levels = "R") +
  gwdegree(0.2, fixed = TRUE) + gwesf_pos(0.6) + gwese_pos(0.6) + gwese_neg(0.6)

quick <- bstergm_sign(f, data = senate, iters = 300, burn.in = 100, seed = 1, verbose = FALSE)

Each iteration runs one auxiliary network simulation per transition and component, started at the observed network. Their length is set by default to three proposals per free dyad (see control.bstergm()). Chains start at a pooled maximum pseudo-likelihood estimate, which also gives the initial proposal covariance; init = "ads" instead runs adaptive direction sampling first, the scheme used by Caimo and Gollini. Results are reproducible with seed, whatever the number of cores.

The analysis below used two chains of 35,000 iterations, with a 10,000 iteration burn-in and the priors of the paper: \(N(-1, 25)\) for the edges parameter and \(N(0, 25)\) for the others. Interpretation

Taken at face value, the formation and persistence of positive ties both show transitivity among friends (\(\text{gwesf}^+ > 0\)), while negative ties whose endpoints share many enemies are unlikely to form or persist (\(\text{gwese}^- < 0\)). Both are in line with strong structural balance. Positive ties are also more likely between Republicans. The posterior probability that each effect is positive summarises the evidence for its direction:

sapply(fit$theta, function(a) round(apply(a, 2, function(v) mean(v > 0)), 3))
#>                         F     P
#> edges               0.000 0.000
#> nodematch.Party.R   0.999 0.996

#> gwesf_pos.fixed.0.6 1.000 1.000
#> gwese_pos.fixed.0.6 0.857 0.040
#> gwese_neg.fixed.0.6 0.000 0.000

The next two sections show that, for this model, these estimates must be read with care.

Goodness of fit

bgof() simulates the formation and persistence networks of each transition from the posterior predictive distribution. It returns the centred cumulative differences \(\sum_t \{s(z'_t) - s(z_t)\}\) of the model statistics, which should be centred at zero for a well-fitting model, together with any other monitored statistics. By default the simulations are five times longer than those used for fitting, so the check is not tied to the fitting length:

gf <- bgof(fit, nsim = 50, seed = 1, verbose = FALSE,
           monitor = ~ degree(0:12) + degree_neg(0:12) + triangle_signed())

Checking the auxiliary simulations

The approximate exchange algorithm treats the end of each auxiliary simulation, which starts at the observed network, as an exact draw from the model. aux_diagnostics() checks this assumption. It runs much longer simulations from the observed networks at the posterior mean and records the model statistics. In the plots, the dashed line is the observed value and the dotted vertical line the length used for fitting:

With three proposals per free dyad the simulated networks stay close to the observed ones. Longer simulations move away from them: in the first formation network, positive ties grow from 380 to about 673, and the negative ties weighted by shared enemies (\(\text{gwese}^-\)) fall from 1918 to about 42. All-negative triangles almost disappear, while unbalanced ++- triangles become far more frequent than observed (see the goodness-of-fit plots). At these parameter values the model’s stationary distribution is far from the data, and the auxiliary simulations used for fitting are far from stationarity. The sampled distribution is then a local, contrastive-divergence-like approximation around the observed networks rather than the posterior of the model.

Longer auxiliary simulations (aux.iters, or control.bstergm(aux.visits =)) move the approximation towards the posterior of the model, at a cost that grows linearly. For models like this one, aux_diagnostics() and bgof() should be checked before the estimates are interpreted, and models less prone to degeneracy should be considered.

The interaction process: binary networks

The interaction layer is a sequence of binary networks and follows an ordinary separable temporal ERGM. Formation networks contain the previous interactions, and persistence networks are contained in them. It is fitted with bstergm() and ordinary ergm terms. bstergm() accepts a signed series, whose interactions it uses, or any list of binary adjacency matrices or network objects, which network_series() converts:

                   gwdegree(0.2, fixed = TRUE) + gwesp(0.6, fixed = TRUE),

Some of these posteriors are as wide as the prior. In these dense networks the pairs that could start interacting already have many shared partners, and all senators have high degrees. On the formation sample space the change statistic of gwesp(0.6) is therefore almost constant and that of gwdegree(0.2) is zero, so these parameters are not identified separately from edges (ergmMPLE() warns about the linear dependence). The draws of edges and gwesp are perfectly correlated:

round(cor(as.matrix(as.mcmc.list(fit_x, components = "F"))), 2)
#>                   edges nodematch.Party.R gwdeg.fixed.0.2 gwesp.fixed.0.6
#> edges              1.00              0.00           -0.01           -1.00
#> nodematch.Party.R  0.00              1.00            0.01           -0.01
#> gwdeg.fixed.0.2   -0.01              0.01            1.00            0.01
#> gwesp.fixed.0.6   -1.00             -0.01            0.01            1.00

Their combination, which gives the probability that a new interaction forms, is well determined, so predictions are not affected, but the separate parameters should not be interpreted. Dropping gwdegree(), or using terms whose change statistics vary on the sample space, avoids the problem.

Simulation and forecasting

simulate() returns posterior predictive replicates of the observed transitions: the formation and persistence networks and the signed networks they imply at each time point, given the observed interactions:

sims <- simulate(fit, nsim = 1, seed = 2)
sapply(sims[[1]]$y, signed_triad_census)
#>     S100 S101
#> +++ 1271 1700
#> ++- 2157 3042
#> +-- 1821 2435

predict() forecasts future networks. A bstergm() fit alone forecasts binary networks:

summary(predict(fit_x, horizon = 1, nsim = 20, seed = 3, verbose = FALSE))

Further options

  • Time-varying covariates: formula can be a list with one formula per transition. Lagged dyadic covariates of the previous network are available as edgecov("lag.pos"), edgecov("lag.neg") and edgecov("lag.active").
  • Adaptive direction sampling: method = "ads" (or aea_ads()) runs a population of interacting chains; init = "ads" uses it only to find starting values for adaptive Metropolis-Hastings.
  • Priors and starting values: prior.mean, prior.sigma and init accept component-specific values as list(F = ..., P = ...). A previous fit can be passed as init to continue its chains.
  • Static signed ERGMs: networks built by as_signed_network() can be used directly with ergm::ergm() and the constraint ~active_signs.
  • Version 0.1 code: aea_amh(), aea_ads() and simulate_stats() accept the arguments of the first version of the package (then called B2Lstergm), including multilayer formulas with the constraint term L(~edges, active). That term is detected and dropped automatically, and the formula is translated into the equivalent native terms when possible.
